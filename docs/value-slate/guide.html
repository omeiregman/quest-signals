The idea in four lines

  1. Bookmakers sometimes put a price on a team that is too generous compared with the real chance of it winning. That is a value bet.
  2. We find the real chance by looking at the sharpest prices in the market (Betfair Exchange, Pinnacle), where professional money keeps prices honest.
  3. Each week you put your budget on a few of those bets, split so that any one winner pays the whole week back.
  4. No single bet is safe. The plan only wins if, over hundreds of bets, the prices you took were really too generous.

Odds and chance

Decimal odds

What you get back for every €1 if the bet wins, including your €1.

Odds 2.50: stake €10, win and you get €25 back (€15 profit).

Implied chance

The chance the odds suggest. Work it out as 1 ÷ odds.

Odds 2.50 → 1 ÷ 2.5 = 40%. Odds 5.00 → 20%.

Bookmaker’s cutmargin, overround, vig

Add up the implied chances of every outcome and you get more than 100%. The extra is the bookmaker’s built-in profit.

A coin flip priced 1.90 / 1.90 → 52.6% + 52.6% = 105.2%. The cut is 5.2%. Big UK bookmakers take about 5–6% on top leagues, 8–12% on small ones.

Fair odds

The odds with the cut taken out, so the chances add up to exactly 100%. This is our best guess at the real chance.

Coin flip 1.90 / 1.90 → fair odds 2.00 / 2.00 (50% each).

Removing the cutde-vig, power method

There are a few ways to take the cut out. Bookmakers hide more of their cut in the long shots, so the method we use (the power method) takes more off the long shots and less off the favourites. A simpler method makes long shots look more likely than they are.

Favourite / long shot

The favourite is the likely winner (short odds, like 1.50). A long shot is unlikely (long odds, like 6.00 or 10.00).

Soft bookmaker

A normal bookmaker for the public: Bet365, bwin, Betsson, 7bet, Topsport. They copy and adjust prices, are sometimes slow, and limit people who win. This is where value bets are found and placed.

Sharp pricereference

A price that professionals bet into heavily, so it is usually very close to the real chance. We use it as the referee.

Pinnacle (a bookmaker that welcomes winners) and Betfair Exchange (people betting against each other). You most likely can’t bet at either from Lithuania, so we only read their prices.

Liquid vs thin market

Liquid: lots of money traded, so the exchange price is reliable (Premier League, Bundesliga). Thin: little money, so the price can be wide or out of date (Scottish League Two, Greek league). We only trust exchange prices when their cut is 3% or less.

Value

Value bet

A bet where the soft bookmaker’s odds are higher than the fair odds.

Fair chance of an away win is 40% (fair odds 2.50). Bet365 offers 2.65. That is a value bet.

Edge

How much better than fair the price is: fair chance × odds − 1.

0.40 × 2.65 − 1 = +6%. On average, every €100 bet like this returns €106. Any single bet still wins €165 or loses €100. The +6% only shows up over many bets.

The long-shot trapfavourite–long-shot bias

Bookmakers overcharge most on long shots, and long-shot prices are the hardest to judge. So “value” flagged at odds of 4 and above is often fake.

A public record of 20,380 real value bets: odds under 4 made +4.4%; odds 4 and above lost 0.7% (and −13% since 2022).

Edge too big to be true

Edges above about 12–15% are usually a wrong or out-of-date price, not a gift. Bookmakers can also cancel bets placed at an obvious mistake. We cap edges at 12% (Short odds) or 30% (the others).

How we check it is real

Backtest

Replaying the past as if we were there: we only use prices that existed before kickoff, place the bets, then look at the real result.

Closing price

The last price just before kickoff. By then all the news and the professional money are in, so it is the most accurate price there is.

Beating the closing priceCLV, closing-line value

Did you get a better price than the final one? If you bought at 2.65 and the fair closing odds were 2.50, you beat it by 6%, win or lose. People who beat the closing price over hundreds of bets make money in the long run. It is the fastest honest test of an edge.

Like buying a share for €95 that closes the day at €100: you did well, whatever happens next.

The ± numberstandard error

How much a result wobbles from luck alone. A result counts as real only when it is more than twice its wobble away from zero.

CLV +1.4% ± 0.7%: 1.4 is twice 0.7, so it is just about real. ROI +24% ± 29%: smaller than its wobble, so it could easily be luck.

Sample size

How many bets the result rests on. To prove a 3% edge from profit alone needs about 9,000–22,000 bets. To prove it with closing prices needs a few hundred. That is why we watch closing prices first.

Out of sample

Testing rules on games they were not built from. Rules shaped on 2025/26 and then checked on 2026/27 are more trustworthy than rules that only look good on the season they came from.

Money words

Bankroll

All the money set aside for betting. In the replay: €1,000.

Weekly budget

How much of the bankroll goes on one week’s slate. Fixed (€20, €50, €100) or a share of the bankroll (2%, 5%, 10%), which shrinks after losses and grows after wins.

Stake / return / profit

Stake: what you put on. Return: what comes back. Profit: return minus stake.

ROIprofit per €1 staked

Profit divided by everything you staked.

Staked €3,100 over the season, profit €749 → ROI +24%.

Worst dropdrawdown

The biggest fall from a high point before recovering.

Up to €1,500, then down to €1,200 before recovering → a 20% drop.

Going brokerisk of ruin

The chance the bankroll runs out. It depends far more on stake size than on the edge. With a real 2% edge and €1,000: €100 a week has about an 18% chance of going broke within three seasons; €50 a week about 2%. About 2% of the bankroll a week (€20 on €1,000) is the size the maths supports.

Your rule, in words

Slate

The group of bets for one week.

One-winner rule

Stakes are split so any single winner returns at least the whole week’s budget. That only works if 1/odds of all the picks add up to 1 or less.

Two picks at 2.00, three at 3.00, five at 5.00, or mixed: 2.00 + 4.00 + 4.00.

Coverage

That sum of 1/odds. Under 1 means one winner pays back more than the budget; exactly 1 means it pays back exactly the budget.

Equal-return staking

Bigger stakes on shorter odds, smaller on longer odds, so every winner pays the same amount.

€100 on 2.00 and 4.00 → €66.67 on the 2.00, €33.33 on the 4.00. Either one winning returns €133.

Wipe-out week

A week where every pick loses. Under the one-winner rule this happens about 1 week in 3, however many games you pick.

The takeaway. With the one-winner rule, more games forces longer odds, so you keep losing everything about 1 week in 3, and those long odds are exactly where value fails. Seven or more games only makes sense at short odds without the one-winner rule: then a total wipe-out becomes rare (7 games at 2.50: under 3%), but you need about 3 winners to get your money back. Either way, the average result is set by the edge, never by the number of games.

More bookmaker accounts

The biggest lever. Lithuania has about seven licensed books (Topsport, Optibet, 7bet, Betsson, OlyBet, Cbet, Twinsbet). Each one you can fund adds prices to compare.

Check prices more often

Value appears for minutes or hours when one bookmaker is slow to move. A price check once a week (what this data has) catches only a fraction. The system today checks twice a day.

More leagues

16 more football leagues (Sweden, Norway, Brazil, USA, Japan and others, many in summer) add about 89 games a week but only about 0.6 extra value bets a week at one bookmaker.

Other sports

Tennis (thousands of matches a month), basketball (NBA, EuroLeague, Lithuanian LKL), ice hockey (NHL, KHL, Swedish and Finnish leagues). Lithuanian bookmakers price basketball heavily. Testing these needs their past odds, which OddsPapi has for 7bet and Betsson.

More markets per game

Asian handicap and other goal lines sit at odds near 2.00, where value holds up best, and show almost no long-shot trap.
